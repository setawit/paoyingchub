# Output Templates and Rubrics

Use these defaults when the user does not provide a stricter format.

## 1. facebook-post

### Default shape
1. Hook
2. Problem, tension, or surprising truth
3. Insight or lesson
4. One focused takeaway or call to action

### Self-check
- Does the first line stop the scroll?
- Can a busy reader understand the point by line 3?
- Is the language simple enough to scan on mobile?
- Is there one main idea, not three competing ones?
- Does the ending invite thought or action without sounding needy?

## 2. speech-meeting

### Default shape
1. Opening story or scene
2. Meaning of the moment
3. Repeated theme line
4. Direction, challenge, or invitation
5. Memorable close

### Self-check
- Does it sound natural aloud?
- Is there a visible emotional arc from current reality to desired future?
- Is repetition used as rhythm, not clutter?
- Can the audience remember one line after hearing it once?
- Does the ending lift energy or resolve tension?

## 3. persuade-decision

### Default shape
1. Start from their goal or pressure
2. Name the real choice
3. Clarify the contrast
4. Support with concise proof
5. Recommend the next decision

### Self-check
- Does it start from their interest rather than the writer's preference?
- Is the trade-off obvious?
- Is the recommendation easy to approve or repeat?
- Is there enough proof to justify urgency?
- Does the wording reduce resistance rather than trigger it?

## 4. pitch-investor-idea

### Default shape
1. Old world
2. Shift or emerging change
3. Problem and wedge
4. Solution and advantage
5. Proof or traction
6. Why now / why us / what comes next

### Self-check
- Is the opportunity framed as a shift, not just a product?
- Does the reveal happen in a controlled sequence?
- Is there a clear wedge rather than broad ambition only?
- Are proof points concrete?
- Does the pitch make the listener want to join the story?

## 5. negotiation-conflict

### Default shape
1. Respectful opening
2. Clear statement of issue
3. Principle, boundary, or constraint
4. Options or path forward
5. Calm close

### Self-check
- Is the message firm without insult?
- Can the other side say yes without losing face?
- Are boundaries specific and enforceable?
- Is the tone lower-temperature than the conflict itself?
- Is the next step practical and unambiguous?

## Default explanation block

After the draft, explain the mechanics briefly using this style:

- Hook / opening choice
- Structure choice
- Persuasion lever used
- Why the ending fits the scenario

Keep explanation short and practical. Focus on cause-and-effect, not theory jargon.

## Optional alternate variants

Only provide variants when they change strategy in a meaningful way, such as:
- softer vs firmer negotiation tone
- visionary vs numbers-first investor pitch
- high-energy vs credibility-first facebook post
- inspirational vs operational meeting speech

Do not generate cosmetic variants that merely swap adjectives.
