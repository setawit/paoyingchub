# DNA Map

Use the names below as shorthand for rhetorical patterns, not for impersonation.

## 1. facebook-post

### DNA blend
- **Tony Robbins** -> opening jolt, emotional voltage, urgency, momentum language
- **Steve Jobs** -> clean wording, sharp takeaway, low-friction readability

### Convert the dna into writing behavior
- Open with a line that interrupts scrolling
- State the point in plain language within the first 2 to 3 lines
- Keep sentences simple enough to understand in one pass
- Build momentum with short bursts, not dense paragraphs
- End with one crisp insight, invitation, or call to action

### Best for
- thought-leadership posts
- launch or announcement posts
- motivation with a business lesson
- opinion posts that need reach without losing clarity

### Avoid
- vague hype with no point
- overexplaining before the hook lands
- polished-but-flat corporate language

## 2. speech-meeting

### DNA blend
- **Obama** -> narrative arc, inclusive framing, sense of shared journey
- **MLK Jr.** -> repetition, cadence, vivid imagery, memorable rhythmic turns

### Convert the dna into writing behavior
- Start with a real or plausible story beat, scene, or observation
- Let the speech travel from present reality to better possibility
- Use repetition intentionally to create spoken rhythm
- Write lines that sound natural out loud, not just on the page
- Leave the audience with a phrase or image worth remembering

### Best for
- town halls
- kickoff meetings
- alignment speeches
- morale or change-management remarks

### Avoid
- bullet-list prose masquerading as a speech
- heavy statistics with no human arc
- repetition that feels ornamental instead of purposeful

## 3. persuade-decision

### DNA blend
- **Dale Carnegie** -> begin with the other person's wants, lower resistance, make ownership feel shared
- **Churchill** -> sharpen the choice with contrast, make the preferred path feel obvious and weighty

### Convert the dna into writing behavior
- Start from their pressure, target, fear, or incentive
- Frame the recommendation so it feels aligned with their goals
- Use contrast to clarify the decision: now vs later, cost vs opportunity, safe-looking vs actually risky
- Remove ego language and direct accusation
- End with a simple decision path or next step

### Best for
- manager approvals
- stakeholder alignment
- partner buy-in
- memo or talking track before a key decision

### Avoid
- leading with what *you* want before establishing their interest
- making the audience feel managed or cornered
- weak contrast that leaves too many interpretations

## 4. pitch-investor-idea

### DNA blend
- **Steve Jobs** -> reveal structure, rule of three, "the world used to work like this, then something changed"
- **Obama** -> larger mission, collective future, invite the audience into something bigger

### Convert the dna into writing behavior
- Frame the old world clearly before introducing the shift
- Reveal the problem, wedge, and solution in a controlled sequence
- Use rule-of-three framing when it improves retention
- Make the opportunity feel both logical and meaningful
- Let the audience imagine themselves participating in the upside

### Best for
- investor pitch script
- demo day narrative
- partnership proposal with venture tone
- visionary internal venture pitch

### Avoid
- feature dumping with no market tension
- vision with no mechanism
- grand claims unsupported by traction or plausible leverage

## 5. negotiation-conflict

### DNA blend
- **Churchill** -> deliberate weight, firm boundaries, strong but controlled wording
- **Carnegie** -> preserve dignity, do not attack identity, allow the other side a path to yes

### Convert the dna into writing behavior
- Open with respect and a shared objective where truthful
- Name the issue clearly without dramatizing it
- Set boundaries in language that is firm, calm, and specific
- Offer options, principles, or next steps that let the other side keep face
- Keep emotional temperature lower than the surrounding conflict

### Best for
- difficult emails
- conflict de-escalation
- negotiation talking points
- refusals that should preserve the relationship

### Avoid
- sarcasm, humiliation, or moral superiority
- vague boundary-setting
- aggressive phrasing that escalates the dispute unnecessarily

## Cross-scenario rule

The user may combine scenarios. In that case, choose the primary scenario by final objective, then borrow only one or two supporting behaviors from a secondary scenario.

Examples:
- investor post on social media -> primary `facebook-post`, borrow reveal logic from `pitch-investor-idea`
- leadership speech asking for a decision -> primary `speech-meeting`, borrow contrast from `persuade-decision`
- hard negotiation after a pitch -> primary `negotiation-conflict`, borrow vision only if it helps de-escalate and align
